Detailed Business Insights
Insight 1: Customer Base and Customer Retention

Key figures from the dashboard:

Total customers: 5,000
Returning customers: 2,262
Analysis

Davey Super Store has a customer base of 5,000 customers, indicating that the business has attracted a considerable number of shoppers.

Of these customers, 2,262 are returning customers. This represents approximately 45.24% of the total customer base.

The remaining 2,738 customers, or 54.76%, are not identified as returning customers in the dashboard.

This means that fewer than half of the customers are classified as returning customers. Although the store has attracted a substantial customer base, the figures suggest an opportunity to strengthen customer loyalty and encourage repeat purchases.

Returning customers are important because they can provide consistent revenue and help the business build long-term relationships with shoppers.

However, the dashboard alone cannot establish why the other customers are not returning. Some may be occasional shoppers, while others may have stopped purchasing. Further analysis would be needed to understand their behaviour.

Business implication

The store should investigate customer purchasing frequency and identify the characteristics of customers who make repeat purchases.

Understanding these patterns could help the business develop more effective customer retention strategies.

Recommendation
Introduce a loyalty programme that rewards customers for repeat purchases.
Offer targeted discounts to customers who have not purchased recently.
Analyse customer purchase history to identify frequent and occasional shoppers.
Investigate whether returning customers spend more than non-returning customers.
Track the returning customer percentage monthly to measure retention performance.

Key takeaway: Davey Super Store has an opportunity to turn more of its existing customers into repeat buyers rather than relying only on attracting new customers.

### Insight 2: Purchase Frequency By Age

<img width="248" height="195" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/988a62eb-edf5-4b6f-83f6-f6eb689f5460" />

Key figures from the dashboard:

Age Group	Purchase Frequency
35–44	5.2K
25–34	5.1K
18–24	3.4K
45–54	2.7K
55–64	0.7K
65+	0.1K

Values are rounded as displayed in the dashboard.

Analysis

The purchase frequency chart shows that customers aged 35–44 have the highest purchase frequency, at approximately 5,200 purchases. Customers aged 25–34 follow closely, with approximately 5,100 purchases.

Together, these two age groups represent the strongest purchasing segments shown in the chart.

Customers aged 18–24 also contribute substantially, with approximately 3,400 purchases. However, purchase frequency decreases among customers aged 45–54 and older.

The 55–64 age group records approximately 700 purchases, while customers aged 65 and above record approximately 100.

This pattern suggests that purchasing activity is concentrated among customers aged 25–44, with customers aged 35–44 forming the leading segment.

Nevertheless, the chart measures purchase frequency, not necessarily the number of unique customers or the amount of revenue generated by each age group.

The lower purchase frequency among older customers also does not automatically mean that these customers are less valuable. Their spending per purchase may differ from that of younger customers.

Business implication

The store can use age-group purchasing patterns to improve marketing campaigns, product recommendations, and promotional offers.

Customers aged 25–44 may be a useful starting point for campaigns designed to encourage frequent purchases.

At the same time, the store should investigate whether other age groups have unmet needs or purchasing preferences that could be addressed.

Recommendation
Focus on high-frequency segments: Develop relevant promotions for customers aged 25–44.
Encourage repeat purchases: Offer loyalty rewards to customers who shop regularly.
Investigate younger customers: Analyse the products and categories purchased most frequently by customers aged 18–24.
Understand older customers: Examine their average order value, preferred product categories, and shopping frequency before designing targeted campaigns.
Compare purchase frequency with revenue: Determine which age groups contribute the most revenue, not just the most transactions.

Key takeaway: Customers aged 25–44 are the strongest purchasing segments by frequency, making them important groups for customer engagement and retention strategies.

###Insight 3: Customer Payment Preferences
<img width="285" height="197" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/922dca8e-4839-4dec-a728-84eb64d11f3a" />
The payment method chart shows the following transaction counts:

Payment Method	Number of Transactions
Credit Card	1,275
Debit Card	841
Digital Wallet	643
Bank Transfer	310
Cash on Delivery	170
Total	3,239
Analysis

Credit cards are the most frequently used payment method, accounting for 1,275 transactions, or approximately 39.36% of the transactions displayed.

Debit cards rank second, with 841 transactions, representing approximately 25.96%.

Digital wallets account for 643 transactions, or approximately 19.85%.

Bank transfers and cash on delivery are used less frequently, recording 310 and 170 transactions respectively.

Combined, credit and debit cards account for approximately 65.33% of the transactions shown.

This indicates that card payments are the dominant payment methods in the displayed data. Digital wallets also contribute a meaningful share, suggesting that electronic payment options are important to the store's customers.

However, the chart shows transaction counts rather than payment values. A payment method with fewer transactions could still generate substantial revenue if its average transaction value is higher.

The dashboard's payment counts also total 3,239, which is lower than the total customer count of 5,000. This is not necessarily an error because customers and transactions are different measures.

Business implication

The store should maintain reliable electronic payment options because cards and digital wallets account for most of the transactions displayed.

Understanding payment preferences may also help the business simplify checkout and reduce unnecessary payment friction.

Recommendation
Ensure credit and debit card payments remain reliable and convenient.
Maintain support for digital wallets because they account for a meaningful proportion of transactions.
Investigate whether payment preferences differ by city, age group, or product category.
Compare average transaction values across payment methods.
Monitor failed transactions and checkout abandonment, if those data are available.

Key takeaway: Credit and debit cards are the dominant payment methods, while digital wallets provide another important payment option.

Analysis

Electronics is the highest-revenue product category, generating approximately $2.0 million.

Home & Garden ranks second at approximately $800,000, followed by Sports at approximately $700,000.

The Electronics category generates substantially more revenue than any other category shown.

Based on the rounded values, Electronics accounts for approximately 46% of the total revenue of $4.31 million. The exact percentage may differ slightly because the category figures are rounded.

Home & Garden and Sports are also important contributors to revenue, although their reported values are considerably lower than Electronics.

At the other end of the chart, Beauty, Food, and Books each generate approximately $100,000.

This difference indicates that revenue generation is concentrated in a relatively small number of product categories.

However, high revenue does not necessarily mean high profitability. Electronics may have higher purchase prices, higher operating costs, or lower profit margins than some other categories.

The dashboard also does not show whether the lower-revenue categories have fewer sales, lower prices, or limited product availability.

Business implication

Electronics is an important category for revenue generation and deserves close attention when planning inventory, pricing, and promotions.

Meanwhile, lower-revenue categories may offer opportunities for improvement, but the store should investigate their sales volumes, margins, and customer demand before making decisions.

Recommendation

For Electronics:

Monitor inventory levels to reduce stockouts of popular products.
Identify the individual products that contribute most to category revenue.
Analyse profit margins to determine whether high revenue translates into strong profitability.
Evaluate whether complementary products can increase average order value.

For Home & Garden and Sports:

Identify their best-selling products.
Test targeted promotional campaigns.
Analyse whether customers purchase these products alongside Electronics.

For lower-revenue categories:

Examine product demand, pricing, stock availability, and sales volumes.
Test promotions on selected products before increasing inventory.
Consider product bundles where customer purchasing patterns support them.

Key takeaway: Electronics is the store's leading revenue category, while lower-revenue categories should be investigated to identify realistic growth opportunities.

###Insight 5: Monthly Sales Trends
<img width="546" height="191" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0ed5d7fa-e574-4a61-9352-bf02e582ac47" />
Key figures from the dashboard:

Month	Sales
January	3,539
February	3,113
March	2,942
Analysis

The monthly sales chart shows a consistent decline in sales from January through March.

January recorded the highest sales, at 3,539. Sales then decreased to 3,113 in February before falling further to 2,942 in March.

Between January and February, sales decreased by 426, representing approximately 12.04%.

Between February and March, sales decreased by a further 171, or approximately 5.49%.

Overall, sales declined by 597 between January and March, representing a decrease of approximately 16.87%.

Although the decline continued across all three months, the rate of decline slowed in March.

This could indicate that sales are approaching a more stable level, but three months of data are not sufficient to establish that a recovery or stabilisation is occurring.

Several factors could explain the decline, including seasonal purchasing patterns, reduced promotions, stock shortages, changes in customer demand, or differences in the number of selling days.

These are possible explanations rather than confirmed causes.

It is also important to note that the chart measures sales volume, while the dashboard reports total revenue separately. Sales volume and revenue can move differently if prices or product mix change.

Business implication

The decline in monthly sales deserves investigation because continued reductions could affect revenue and inventory planning.

The business should determine whether the decline is concentrated in specific products, cities, customer groups, or payment methods.

It should also compare sales with the same months in the previous year, if historical data are available, to distinguish a potential business problem from a normal seasonal pattern.

Recommendation
Investigate why sales declined between January and March.
Compare monthly sales by product category to identify which categories contributed most to the decrease.
Analyse sales by city to determine whether the decline is concentrated in specific locations.
Review promotions, pricing changes, stock availability, and customer purchasing patterns.
Track monthly sales alongside revenue and average order value.
Compare year-over-year performance before concluding that the business is experiencing a sustained decline.

Key takeaway: Sales decreased by approximately 16.87% between January and March, making the downward trend one of the most important findings to investigate.
